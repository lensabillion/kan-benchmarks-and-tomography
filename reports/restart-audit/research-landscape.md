# Research Landscape and Experiment Choices

**Search date:** 2026-09-21. This is a targeted primary-source review to guide the restart, not a systematic review or a proof of novelty. The search covered KAN representation and training, fair MLP comparisons, learned spline activations, optical detector tomography, neural preprocessing/reconstruction, and recent KAN quantum tomography. Search phrases included “KAN MLP fair comparison,” “spline KAN equivalence,” “KAN approximation learning guarantees,” “quantum detector tomography neural networks,” and “KAN quantum state detector tomography.”

The analysis uses papers, publisher records, and official code. Search snippets were used for discovery; secondary commentary and informal social posts were not used to establish findings. Where only an abstract or publisher record was available, the table limits its claims accordingly. Author-reported improvements are not independently replicated here. Preprints are identified as such. Local copies of Zhang's article and supplement were read because online supplementary binary access failed.

## KANs, Splines, and Comparison Methodology

| Primary source | What it contributes | What it changes for this project |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Liu et al., *KAN: Kolmogorov–Arnold Networks* (2024; revised paper)](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.19756v5) and [official pykan code](https://github.com/KindXiaoming/pykan) | Edge-wise learned functions, grid refinement, selected scientific benchmarks, and approximation results with smooth-composition assumptions | Reproduce a specified version and recipe. Do not transfer an existence approximation bound into a guarantee about optimization or every real dataset. Modern pykan and the vendored implementation are different audit objects. |
| [Yu, Yu and Wang, *KAN or MLP: A Fairer Comparison* (2024)](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.16674v2) and [KANbeFair](https://github.com/yu-rp/KANbeFair) | Parameter/FLOP comparisons and spline-activation ablations across task families | The architecture-versus-activation question is established prior work. Borrow its useful controls while repairing test-driven selection, the local metric implementation and parameter accounting. Its experiments do not prove the thesis's exact function-class equality. |
| [Liu et al., *KAN 2.0: Kolmogorov–Arnold Networks Meet Science* (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10205) | Multiplication nodes and workflows for symbolic structure and scientific knowledge | A low-error vanilla KAN regressor is not equivalent to demonstrated symbolic discovery. If interpretation becomes the goal, define an expression-recovery experiment and distinguish architectures. |
| [Shukla et al., *A comprehensive and FAIR comparison between MLP and KAN representations for differential equations and operator networks* (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02917) | Scientific-learning comparisons of accuracy, computation and basis choices | There is already substantial work on when spline KANs are efficient. Its PDE/operator setting differs from these regression tasks; use it to motivate controls, not to import its ranking. |
| [Poeta et al., *A Benchmarking Study of Kolmogorov–Arnold Networks on Tabular Data* (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.14529) and [official benchmark code](https://github.com/eleonorapoeta/benchmarking-KAN) | Reports competitive tabular classification under its protocol, with training-cost tradeoffs | Findings differ from some broader benchmark narratives. A housing notebook is insufficient evidence that KAN flexibility is universally a liability on real data. Target/task and tuning protocol must accompany every ranking. |
| [Zeng et al., *KAN versus MLP on Irregular or Noisy Functions* (2024 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07906) | Compares six regularity/noise categories while controlling parameters and sample size; reports no universal KAN winner | Already present in the local paper library. Its scope motivates an explicit noise/regularity axis, rather than explaining every observed ranking by whether data are “clean” or “messy.” Abstract reviewed online; no independent replication here. |
| [Kratsios, Kim and Furuya, *Approximation Rates in Besov Norms and Sample-Complexity of Kolmogorov–Arnold Networks with Residual Connections* (2025, v3)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15110v3) | Approximation and statistical bounds for specified residual KAN classes and regularity assumptions | Useful for a rigorous theory section. It does not establish convergence of the optimizers used here or equality of the thesis's single-layer classes. The current title differs from older indexed versions. |
| [Southworth et al., *Multilevel Training for Kolmogorov Arnold Networks* (March 2026 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04827v1) | Spline-KAN to multichannel power-ReLU change of basis, optimization geometry, and multilevel knot training | Directly relevant to both the novelty claim and the grid-refinement hypothesis. Equivalent forward representations can train differently. Study coefficient transport and equal work before attributing performance to architecture. |
| [Bagrow and Bongard, *Optimized Architectures for Kolmogorov–Arnold Networks* (2025 preprint; April 2026 revision)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12448v2) | Explores depth, sparsification, deep supervision and description-length objectives | A useful later comparator if architecture search remains central. It increases the need to specify what is actually new; importing its complexity now would distract from numerical correctness. |
| [Vecci, Piazza and Uncini, *Learning and Approximation Capabilities of Adaptive Spline Activation Function Neural Networks* (1998)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0893608097001184) | Earlier adaptive spline activations using learned control points | Learned spline activations predate KAN. The historical framing should acknowledge this family instead of implying that learned nonlinearities began in 2024. Publisher abstract reviewed. |
| [Scardapane et al., *Learning activation functions from data using cubic spline interpolation* (2016 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.05509) | Per-neuron adaptive activations with local spline interpolation and damping | Relevant precedent for spline MLPs and explicit control of oscillation. It is a different spline parameterization, so similarity is not exact model identity. |
| [Agarwal et al., *Neural Additive Models* (2020 preprint; NeurIPS 2021)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13912v2) | Neural feature-wise functions in an additive interpretable model | Provides a useful interpretability and additive-function baseline. Edge readability is not unique to KAN, and interactions require a separate structural argument. |
| [Cawley and Talbot, *On Over-fitting in Model Selection and Subsequent Selection Bias in Performance Evaluation* (2010)](https://jmlr.org/papers/v11/cawley10a.html) | Explains how model selection itself can overfit a finite evaluation criterion | Direct motivation for nested or independent validation, symmetric tuning budgets and locked final test data. A minimum over many test scores is not an unbiased estimate of a training procedure's performance. |

The unresolved local question is therefore not whether someone has tried spline activations, nor whether a KAN can sometimes fit scientific functions. It is whether **a specified combination of edge placement, coefficient sharing, residual terms, grids and optimization offers a reproducible advantage for a specified target structure and budget**. Additive, compositional, ridge-interaction and rotated-target controls distinguish those possibilities more cleanly than more unstructured datasets.

## Detector Tomography and Neural Reconstruction

| Primary source | Established result or scope | Relevance and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Fiurášek, *Maximum-likelihood estimation of quantum measurement* (2001)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0101027) | Infers physical measurement operators from probe data through likelihood-based estimation | Physical detector reconstruction has alternatives to unregularized inversion and this particular SDP. It does not supply evidence for a KAN speedup. |
| [Lundeen et al., *Tomography of quantum detectors* (2009)](https://www.nature.com/articles/nphys1133) | Experimental POVM characterization of photon detectors | Establishes the detector-tomography setting and why estimating response probabilities at observed probes is not the entire inverse problem. |
| [Feito et al., *Measuring Measurement: Theory and Practice* (2009)](https://arxiv.org/abs/0906.3440) | Detector reconstruction, ill-conditioning, and smoothing regularization | The regularized physical estimator must be a strong, tuned baseline. A smoothing effect is not automatically KAN-specific. |
| [Zhang et al., *Mapping coherence in measurement via full quantum tomography of a hybrid optical detector* (2012)](https://www.nature.com/articles/nphoton.2012.107) | Phase-sensitive weak-homodyne detector data and a recursive band reconstruction strategy | This is the source experiment. Its norm, outcome handling, phase convention and regularizer must be distinguished from notebook modifications. Main article and supplement were read locally; online data bytes were not independently hash-verified. |
| [Palmieri et al., *Experimental neural network enhanced quantum tomography* (2020)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41534-020-0248-6) | Supervised neural filtering of tomography data under experimental imperfections | Neural preprocessing has precedent. Its noisy-to-ideal training setup differs from fitting one measured response surface to itself, so the paper does not validate this repository's denoising claim. |
| [*Tomography of quantum detectors using neural networks* (2023)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2405896323004317) | Neural detector reconstruction and numerical phase-insensitive examples | Directly relevant prior work against broad “no prior neural detector reconstruction” claims. The publisher abstract does not establish exact constraint handling, speed or applicability to the current phase-sensitive setting. |
| [Torlai et al., *Neural-network quantum state tomography* (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41567-018-0048-5) | Neural reconstruction of quantum states; cited in the thesis | Relevant neighboring literature, but the unknown is the state rather than a measurement operator. The thesis's citation cannot stand in for a review of neural detector tomography. |
| [Wu et al., *Sparsified Kolmogorov–Arnold Networks for Interpretable Quantum State Tomography* (June 2026 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.11814) | A controlled three-qubit GHZ-family state reconstruction study with sparse-pathway and symbolic-structure checks | Rules out an unqualified “first KAN quantum tomography” claim as of this audit. It does **not** establish priority over the exact optical-detector pipeline here. Its constrained family and known Pauli structure also limit generalization. |

No exact prior KAN-smoother-to-Zhang-APD pipeline was located in this bounded search. That is an unresolved novelty question, not a positive proof of priority. A defensible novelty statement must specify the unknown object, probe family, data regime, learned role, physical constraints, evaluation protocol, and advantage over simpler alternatives.

## Alternatives Worth Testing

| Candidate | Why it belongs in the comparison | Main limitation to measure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Raw regularized joint reconstruction | Uses the known physical forward map directly, with no neural fitting stage | Solver cost and sensitivity to calibration, cutoff and regularization |
| Raw recursive reconstruction | Closest to the source method and current pipeline | Quadrature, accumulated band errors and the distinction between coefficient count and PSD-cone cost |
| Periodic Fourier/spline smoothing followed by the same inverse solver | Strong inexpensive baseline with explicit phase structure | Bias from basis truncation or over-smoothing real coherence |
| Plain MLP and spline MLP followed by the same inverse solver | Tests whether learned smoothness or spline basis explains the effect | Training variability, effective capacity and the correct periodic/input-domain handling |
| KAN followed by the same inverse solver | Existing implementation direction; inspectable component curves | Whether any benefit survives shared data, tuning, loss normalization and total-time accounting |
| Regularized unconstrained estimate, with optional physical projection/refit | Separates conditioning control from feasibility enforcement | Projection can worsen fit; unconstrained estimate may remain unphysical |
| Direct physically parameterized estimator | Can enforce valid effects without optimizing directly over a PSD cone | May be nonconvex, expensive to differentiate, or sensitive to initialization; not automatically faster |

For a binary effect, a Hermitian matrix mapped through a matrix sigmoid has eigenvalues in $(0,1)$. Another construction forms positive matrices $A_n=B_nB_n^\dagger$ and normalizes them with $S^{-1/2}A_nS^{-1/2}$, $S=\sum_nA_n$, when $S$ is positive definite. These are mathematical parameterizations, not claims of a new method or proven computational superiority. Boundary effects, conditioning and multi-outcome completeness need explicit treatment. The point is that failure of unrestricted least squares does not make a full SDP the only possible physical reconstruction.

## Proposed Contribution and Stop Conditions

The clearest applied study is a controlled assessment of learned surface priors for sparse or photon-limited detector tomography. Prespecify a primary metric and practical improvement threshold; compare matched measurement budgets, identical outer splits, properly normalized reconstruction objectives, failure rates and complete computation costs. Use known synthetic operators to separate physical recovery from predictive fitting, then evaluate real data without calling any fitted estimate ground truth.

Continue a KAN-specific contribution only if it adds a reproducible advantage over ordinary periodic smoothers and the spline MLP, or yields independently verified scientific structure. If those models tie, a finding about generic smoothing and regularization is still useful. If the advantage disappears when the baseline is corrected, publish that diagnostic rather than expanding a confounded experiment.

The theory track can become a precise account of routed representations, depth, coefficient sharing and training geometry. It should not be organized around rescuing an equality that the explicit counterexample refutes. The benchmark track can establish when target compositional structure and grid adaptation matter, using rotations and equal-work ablations. These are narrower, testable questions that fit the existing assets and current literature.
